## How I Discovered This

My grandmother made these every Sunday. I didn't know they were special — I thought all cookies were this easy. It wasn't until I was an adult, trying to recreate them, that I realized she'd been holding down a genius simple recipe my entire life.

She used supermarket peanut butter, didn't measure anything precisely, and always said "you can't mess up cookies if you don't overbake them." She was right.

These are the cookies I make when I want something sweet but don't want to think about it. They take twenty minutes, they use five ingredients I always have, and nobody ever leaves without asking for the recipe.

I make these more than any other cookie now. They're my "I need to bring something to the potluck" cookie, my "the kids need a snack" cookie, my "I need something to go with milk" cookie. Simple and reliable.

## The Real Recipe

### Ingredients

- 1 cup peanut butter (natural, stirred)
- ½ cup sugar (plus more for rolling)
- 1 egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- ½ teaspoon baking soda

### Instructions

Preheat oven to 350°F (175°C).

Mix everything together until smooth. Roll into 1-inch balls. Place on ungreased baking sheet. Press with a fork in a crisscross pattern. Bake 8-10 minutes — edges should be lightly golden, centers will look underdone. They'll set as they cool.

Let cool on the sheet for 5 minutes, then transfer to a rack.

## Ingredient Deep Dive

**Natural Peanut Butter:** The critical ingredient. Look for only peanuts and salt in the ingredients. The natural oils separate — that's normal and desired. Stir well before measuring. No hydrogenated oils, no added sugar.

**Sugar:** Plain white sugar works perfectly here. The cookies aren't trying to be complex — they're trying to be simple and delicious. The rolling sugar adds that sweet crunch to the exterior.

**Egg:** Provides structure and binding. One large egg is all you need for this ratio.

**Vanilla Extract:** Adds warmth and complexity. Use real extract, not artificial — the flavor difference is significant.

**Baking Soda:** The leavener. Creates the slight spread and soft texture. Don't skip this — without it, you'd have peanut butter discs, not cookies.

---

## Chef Tips

**1. Use natural peanut butter.** The kind with just peanuts and salt. No hydrogenated oils, no sugar. It tastes better and bakes better. Stir it well before using.

**2. Don't overbake.** This is the biggest mistake. They should look slightly underdone when you pull them. They'll firm up as they cool. Better underdone than dry.

**3. Room temperature ingredients blend easier.** Cold peanut butter is thick and clumpy. Let everything sit out 20 minutes before starting.

**4. The fork matters.** Pressing the dough creates the signature look AND flattens the cookies for even baking. Use one with good tines.

**5. Double the batch.** Seriously. You'll regret only making one dozen. These disappear fast.

**6. Use a cookie scoop:** For uniform cookies, a small scoop ensures even baking and consistent size.

**7. Line the baking sheet:** Parchment paper prevents sticking and makes cleanup easy.

## Timing Guide

- **Prep Time:** 10 minutes (mixing and shaping)
- **Bake Time:** 8-10 minutes
- **Cool Time:** 5 minutes on sheet + 15 minutes on rack
- **Total Time:** 30-35 minutes

This is genuinely a 20-minute recipe from start to finish.

## Common Mistakes

**Overbaking:** The single biggest mistake. These should look underdone when you remove them. They'll set as they cool. Overbaked = dry and crumbly.

**Using processed peanut butter:** The hydrogenated oils affect texture and flavor. Natural is the only way to go.

**Cold ingredients:** Cold peanut butter won't mix smoothly. Let everything come to room temperature first.

**Not pressing enough:** The crisscross serves a purpose — it flattens the cookie for even baking. Don't be tentative with the fork.

**Skipping the sugar roll:** Rolling in sugar isn't just decorative — it adds texture and sweetness to the exterior.

## Serving Ideas

**1. Milk Dunking:** The classic. These are built for dunking in cold milk.

**2. Ice Cream Sandwich:** Flatten slightly larger, add a scoop of vanilla ice cream between two cookies.

**3. Chocolate Dip:** Dip half in melted chocolate for an extra treat.

**4. Trail Mix Addition:** Crumble into trail mix for extra protein.

---

## Storage Notes

**Shelf Life:** 1 week in an airtight container

**Storage Method:** Airtight container at room temperature

**Note:** These stay soft for about a week. After that, they start to dry out. Freeze for longer storage — they defrost perfectly.

**Freezer Tip:** These freeze beautifully. Layer between wax paper in a freezer bag for up to 3 months.

## Where It Comes From

Peanut butter cookies are solidly American, born from the Great Depression when cooks needed to make five ingredients do what twelve used to do.

The peanut butter itself is an American invention — invented in the 1890s, popularized in the 1920s. The cookies followed quickly because the combination is obvious: shelf-stable peanut butter + sugar + eggs + leavener = cookies.

The crisscross fork pattern is partly visual and partly functional — it flattens the dough for even baking. Some food historians say it's also just what people did because the fork was handy. Either way, it's iconic now.

## Easy Variations

**Chocolate Chip:** Add ½ cup chocolate chips to the dough.

**Honey Sweet:** Replace sugar with ½ cup honey — they'll be softer and chewier.

**Crunchy:** Use chunky peanut butter instead of smooth.

**Peanut Butter Blossom:** Press a chocolate cup (like a Reese's cup) into the center immediately after removing from the oven.

**No-Fork Option:** Roll into balls and slightly flatten with your palm for a different look — works equally well.
