## How I Discovered This

I found this on a road trip through Mexico — a beach town restaurant where lunch was just-caught fish tacos, and the green sauce on top was this incredible avocado lime cream. I asked for the recipe and the cook, a woman named Elena (yes, name coincidence), pointed at a blender and said "avocado, lime, cilantro, garlic, oil. Don't overthink."

She was right. There's nothing complicated about this — but there are a few things that make it shine versus turn brown and bitter. I learned by watching her work: plenty of lime juice, don't skip the herbs, and blend until silky. That's the whole secret.

I made this nearly every day during that trip and the days following. It became my secret weapon for elevating basic meals. Drizzled over grilled fish, tossed with pasta, as a base for grain bowls — it transformed everything it touched. I've made a thousand versions since, but the core ratio remains true to what Elena showed me.

## The Real Recipe

### Ingredients

- 1 ripe avocado (haas preferred)
- ¼ cup fresh lime juice (about 2 limes)
- ½ cup water (plus more to thin)
- ¼ cup extra virgin olive oil
- 1 cup fresh cilantro (stems and all)
- 1 small garlic clove
- ½ teaspoon salt
- ¼ teaspoon cumin

### Instructions

Throw everything in a blender. Blend until completely smooth. Add more water to reach pourable consistency. Taste and adjust lime or salt.

---

## Ingredient Deep Dive

**Haas Avocados:** The dark, bumpy-skinned variety. These are denser and creamier than the smooth-skinned Florida avocados. Look for ones that give slightly when pressed but aren't mushy. Too firm means underripe; too soft means overripe and possibly bitter.

**Fresh Lime Juice:** Essential. The citric acid does three things: adds bright flavor, helps create creaminess, and prevents browning. Never skip this, and never use bottled.

**Cilantro:** Leaves and tender stems — the woody stems are too fibrous. This contributes the fresh, herbaceous notes that make this "Mexican" rather than just green sauce.

**Extra Virgin Olive Oil:** Adds richness and helps the dressing emulsify smoothly. The peppery notes of quality olive oil complement the avocado without overwhelming.

**Garlic:** One clove is enough. It provides savory depth without dominating the bright lime and cilantro.

**Cumin:** Just a quarter teaspoon adds warmth and depth that elevates this from "green sauce" to "complex sauce."

---

## Chef Tips

**1. Pick the right avocado.** Haas avocados — the ones with bumpy dark skin — are perfect. Too ripe means brown spots and bitter flavor. Firm but gives slightly is ideal.

**2. Lime juice is your friend.** Three reasons: flavor, creaminess, and it prevents browning. Don't skip it. Fresh is better than bottled.

**3. Add water progressively.** You can always thin it out, but you can't thicken it. Start with ¼ cup, blend, add more as needed.

**4. The herbs are flexible.** Love cilantro? Use more. Hate it? Swap for parsley or basil. The avocado-lime base is the star.

**5. It browns — fast.** Press plastic wrap directly onto the surface before refrigerating. It'll buy you an extra day. But honestly? Use it same-day for best results.

**6. Pass through a sieve for extra smoothness:** If your blender isn't powerful, strain the dressing for a silky texture.

**7. Use an immersion blender for small batches:** Works great and creates less cleanup.

## Common Mistakes

**Using the wrong avocado:** Florida avocados are too watery. Hass is the only choice for this dressing.

**Skipping the lime juice:** Without it, the avocado browns within hours and the flavor is flat. Fresh lime is essential.

**Adding too much water at once:** You can't un-thick this. Add water progressively and blend between additions.

**Using stale cilantro:** Fresh cilantro is essential. If it's wilted or yellowed, the whole dressing suffers.

**Over-blending:** Extended blending can heat the mixture andaccelerate browning. Pulse rather than continuous blend.

## Serving Ideas

**1. Fish Tacos:** The original application. Drizzle generously over grilled or fried fish tacos with cabbage and crema.

**2. Grain Bowl Base:** Use as the saucE instead of tahini or ranch for grain bowls with roasted vegetables and protein.

**3. Veggie Dip:** Serve with crudité for a healthier alternative to ranch or blue cheese.

**4. Grilled Chicken Glaze:** Apply in the last minute of grilling for a flavor boost.

---

## Storage Notes

**Shelf Life:** 24-48 hours in the refrigerator

**Storage Method:** Airtight container with plastic wrap pressed directly onto surface

**Note:** This browns quickly due to the avocado. The lime juice slows but doesn't stop browning. For best results, use within the same day. If storing longer, squeeze extra lime juice on top and press wrap directly onto the surface before refrigerating.

**Pro Tip:** Recipe scaling is tricky with this one. Make smaller batches more frequently rather than a large batch that will go bad.

## Where It Comes From

This dressing is a love child of two food cultures: Mexican and Californian fusion.

In Mexico, avocado sauces (salsas verdes) have been around forever — mol verde, guacamole variants, everything with cilantro and lime. The American California cuisine movement picked this up in the 1980s and ran with it, adding olive oil for richness and making it more of a dressing than a salsa.

What makes this specifically "Californian Mexican" is the approach: treat avocados not just as a dip, but as a base for something creamy and adaptable. It's the same reason you see avocado toast everywhere now —加州 avocado, meet Mediterranean olive oil, Mexican lime.

## Easy Variations

**Spicier:** Add ½ jalapeño (seeded) or ¼ teaspoon cayenne.

**Oil-Free:** Skip the olive oil, add more water or a splash of almond milk.

**Herb Swap:** Replace cilantro with basil for an Italian twist, or parsley for neutral flavor.
