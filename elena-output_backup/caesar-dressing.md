## How I Discovered This

I first understood what Caesar dressing was supposed to taste like in an unexpected place: a border town restaurant in Tijuana. It was 2 AM, I'd wandered in hungry, and the salad they brought out was the best I'd ever had — garlicky, tangy, rich, with a subtle depth I couldn't identify.

The server told me about the anchovy. Of course. Everything in Mexican cooking has a little fish for umami — the Worcestershire, the anchovy paste. Once you know, you can't unknow.

I went home and rebuilt it from scratch. No bottles. No shortcuts. And it's not even hard — it's just about knowing what goes in.

That first homemade batch was revelatory. The store-bought versions I'd grown up with suddenly tasted like pale approximations. The homemade version had depth, complexity, and that subtle umami undertone that elevates rather than overwhelms. I've made it from scratch ever since.

## The Real Recipe

### Ingredients

- 1 cup mayonnaise
- 3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice (about 1 large lemon)
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 2 teaspoons Dijon mustard
- 2 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce
- 1 teaspoon anchovy paste (or 2 minced anchovy fillets)
- 2 large garlic cloves, minced
- ½ cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese
- ½ teaspoon salt
- ¼ teaspoon black pepper

### Instructions

Whisk everything together until smooth. That's it. Let it sit 30 minutes if you can — the garlic softens and flavors meld.

---

## Ingredient Deep Dive

**Mayonnaise:** The base. This is where the creaminess comes from. Use full-fat — there's no substitution here. Low-fat mayo is watery and tastes like nothing.

**Fresh Lemon Juice:** Adds brightness and acid. Fresh is essential — the brightness is different from bottled. One large lemon yields 3 tablespoons.

**Olive Oil:** Adds richness and helps with the emulsion. A light touch of olive oil works here — you don't want it tasting like salad dressing.

**Dijon Mustard:** Acts as the emulsifier and adds subtle heat. This is technical as well as flavorful — it helps everything combine smoothly.

**Worcestershire Sauce:** This is the umami secret. Made from fermented anchovies, it adds depth without fishiness. Many brands are vegetarian, but traditional contains anchovies.

**Anchovy Paste (or Fillets):** The second umami source. You won't taste fish — you'll taste depth. The salt and funk deepen the overall flavor profile. This is non-negotiable for the authentic version.

**Garlic:** Two large cloves provide significant garlic flavor. The intensity mellows with resting.

**Parmesan Cheese:** Adds saltiness, creaminess, and that characteristic umami richness. Freshly grated is essential — the pre-grated stuff doesn't melt as well.

---

## Chef Tips

**1. Don't use low-fat mayo.** The fat is what makes it rich. This isn't the place to skimp. Full-fat or nothing.

**2. Anchovy is not optional.** You won't taste fish. You'll taste depth. That's the umami magic. If you're vegetarian, skip it — the dressing is still good, just not traditional.

**3. Drizzle the olive oil slowly.** Not into the mix all at once. Slow and steady keeps the emulsion smooth.

**4. Fresh garlic is better.** Yes, it's stronger. Yes, it's worth the effort. Pre-minced from a jar works in a pinch, but fresh makes a real difference.

**5. Let it rest.** Thirty minutes minimum, overnight is better. The garlic softens, the Parmesan incorporates, the flavors become one thing instead of many.

**6. Freshly grate the Parmesan:** Pre-grated cheese contains additives that affect texture. Use a microplane for the finest grate.

**7. Taste and adjust salt:** Different Parmesan brands have different salt levels. Always taste first, then adjust.

---

## Timing Guide

- **Prep Time:** 10 minutes (whisking everything together)
- **Rest Time:** 30 minutes minimum, overnight preferred
- **Total Time (active):** 10 minutes
- **Total Time (including rest):** 40 minutes to overnight

The active time is minimal. The magic happens during the resting, when the garlic mellows and flavors meld.

---

## Common Mistakes

**Using low-fat mayo:** This ruins the dressing. The fat carries flavor and creates the creamy texture. There's no viable low-fat替代.

**Skipping the anchovy:** The "fishy" concern is misplaced — you won't taste fish, you'll taste depth. This is the difference between good Caesar and great Caesar.

**Adding all the oil at once:** This breaks the emulsion and results in a thin, separated dressing.

**Not resting:** Freshly made, the garlic is too sharp and the flavors haven't come together. Resting is essential.

**Using pre-grated Parmesan:** The additives change the texture. Fresh-grated is the only way to get that silky finish.

**Oversalting:** The Parmesan and anchovy are both salty. Add salt conservatively and taste first.

## Serving Ideas

**1. Classic Caesar Salad:** The obvious choice. Toss with chopped romaine and extra Parmesan, croutons.

**2. Pasta Salad:** Toss with penne, cherry tomatoes, and grilled chicken for a protein-rich lunch.

**3. Seafood Dip:** This is incredible as a dip for grilled shrimp or crab cakes.

**4. grilled Steak Topping:** Spread on ciabatta with grilled steak and arugula for an elevated sandwich.

---

## Storage Notes

**Shelf Life:** 1 week in the refrigerator

**Storage Method:** Sealed container

**Note:** The garlic continues intensifying over time. This is one dressing that genuinely improves after 24 hours, as the flavors have a chance to meld completely. However, after a week, the garlic becomes too strong. Use within 5-7 days.

**Pro Tip:** The flavor peaks around 48 hours. Make this on a Sunday for the work week.

## Where It Comes From

Caesar Cardini invented this dressing at his restaurant in Tijuana, Mexico, in 1924. He's credited with the original — a simple creation: egg, olive oil, garlic, lemon, Worcestershire, Parmesan, romaine.

The story goes that Cardini created it for a Fourth of July crowd when supplies ran low. He made what he had — and it became one of the most famous dressings in American history.

The American version got heavier on the mayo (safety and richness), added the mustard (emulsification), and sometimes dropped the egg (pasteurized mayo replaced it). The Mexican border version often stays closer to the original.

What's consistent: the garlic, lemon, Worcestershire, and Parmesan. Everything else is interpretation.

---

## Easy Variations

**Vegetarian:** Skip the anchovy paste. Add 1 extra teaspoon Worcestershire (most brands are vegan).

**Lighter:** Swap half the mayo for Greek yogurt. Still rich but with more protein.

**Dairy-Free:** Replace Parmesan with nutritional yeast. Different, but gets the cheesy-nutty profile.
