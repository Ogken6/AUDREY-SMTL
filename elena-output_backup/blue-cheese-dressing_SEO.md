# Blue Cheese Dressing Recipe

*The salad-topping legend, creamier than you remember.*

---

This **blue cheese dressing** is the real deal — creamier than you remember, with real cheese chunks instead of that pale grocery store version. Once you make it at home, you'll never go back to bottled.

I get it — blue cheese is an acquired taste. I didn't love it until I was about twenty-three, eating Buffalo wings at a sports bar with friends. The wings were good, but the **blue cheese dressing** was the revelation. The creaminess, the tang, the chunks of actual cheese instead of that pale grocery store version.

I asked the bartender how they made it. He said "we just mix it up — blue cheese, sour cream, mayo, little garlic, vinegar." That's it. Five ingredients, no shortcuts needed. I went home and recreated it that week, and I've never bought the bottled version since. Not once.

---

## The Real Recipe

### Ingredients

- ½ cup crumbled blue cheese (about 4 ounces)
- ½ cup sour cream
- ¼ cup mayonnaise
- ¼ cup buttermilk
- 1 tablespoon white wine vinegar
- 1 small garlic clove, minced
- ¼ teaspoon salt
- ¼ teaspoon black pepper

### Instructions

Whisk sour cream, mayo, buttermilk, vinegar, garlic, salt, and pepper until smooth. Gently fold in the blue cheese crumbles. That's it. Don't overmix — leave some chunks.

---

## Ingredient Deep Dive

**Blue Cheese Crumbles:** The star. Pre-crumbled cheese is often dry and less flavorful. Buy a wedge and crumble it yourself — you'll taste the difference immediately. Roquefort (France), Gorgonzola (Italy), and Danish Blue are all excellent choices.

**Sour Cream:** Provides the tangy base and creamy texture. Full-fat is non-negotiable here. Low-fat versions are too watery and lack the richness.

**Mayonnaise:** Adds body and richness. The fat content helps create that velvety mouthfeel that makes this dressing feel indulgent.

**Buttermilk:** The secret ingredient. It's thinner than cream and provides that signature tang. The cultures in buttermilk also help the dressing emulsify better. Don't substitute with regular milk.

**White Wine Vinegar:** Adds brightness without coloring the dressing. A little goes a long way — you're looking for notes of tang, not a vinegar punch.

**Garlic:** Just one small clove. Enough to add depth without dominating the blue cheese flavor.

---

## Chef Tips

**1. Keep everything cold.** Cold ingredients emulsify better and the blue cheese holds its shape. Take everything out of the fridge 15 minutes before, not longer.

**2. Don't crush the cheese.** Fold it in gently. You're not making a smoothie. Chunky bites are the whole point.

**3. Quality cheese matters.** Pre-crumbled blue cheese is often dry and less flavorful. Buy a chunk and crumble it yourself. Good options: Roquefort, Gorgonzola, Danish Blue.

**4. It thickens in the fridge.** Expect it to firm up overnight. Add a splash of buttermilk to thin it back out when serving.

**5. Buttermilk is key.** It's thinner than cream and gives that signature tang. Don't swap for regular milk unless you have to — the funk is the point.

**6. Taste as you go:** The cheese varies in saltiness significantly between brands. Start with less salt, taste, and adjust.

**7. Let it rest before serving:** The flavors meld and improve significantly after 30 minutes in the fridge.

---

## Timing Guide

- **Prep Time:** 10 minutes (whisking and folding)
- **Rest Time:** 30 minutes (for flavors to meld)
- **Total Time (active):** 10 minutes
- **Total Time (including rest):** 40 minutes

This is a quick dressing to make — the active time is just stirring and folding. The resting time makes a significant difference in the final flavor.

---

## Common Mistakes

**Over-mixing the blue cheese:** This is the #1 mistake. You're not making a smoothie. Some chunks should remain. Over-mixing turns this into a plain creamy sauce, losing the textural interest.

**Using low-fat dairy:** The fat is half the point. Skimping results in a watery, tasteless dressing that can't compete with bold wing flavors.

**Skipping the resting time:** Taste this immediately after making versus 30 minutes later, and you'll understand why resting matters. The flavors meld and deepen considerably.

**Not crushing fresh cheese:** Pre-crumbled cheese is often dry, dusty, and less flavorful. You need a wedge for the full experience.

**Adding salt before tasting:** Different blue cheeses have drastically different salt levels. Always taste first, then adjust.

---

## Serving Ideas

**1. Wedge Salad (The Classic):** A simple wedge of iceberg lettuce, topped with dressing, croutons, and extra blue cheese. This is the original application.

**2. Buffalo Wings Dip:** The sports bar classic. Serve alongside hot wings with celery sticks. It's the reason for the season.

**3. Veggie Dip:** Raw bell peppers, carrots, and broccoli taste incredible with this. A healthier way to enjoy it.

**4. Burger Topping:** Spread on a burger bun in place of pickles and sauce. The tang cuts through the richness of the meat.

---

## Storage Notes

**Shelf Life:** 5-7 days in the refrigerator

**Storage Method:** Sealed container in the fridge

**Note:** This will thicken significantly overnight due to the dairy. Add a splash of buttermilk when serving to thin it back to the right consistency. The blue cheese flavor continues developing over time.

**Pro Tip:** The flavor actually improves after 24 hours once the dairy and cheese have truly melded. Make it a day ahead for the best results.

## Where It Comes From

Here's the thing: no one actually knows where blue cheese dressing originated. It emerged in American steakhouse culture sometime in the early-to-mid 1900s, likely as a salad topping for wedge salads.

Some food historians point to the original Waldorf salad (celery, apples, walnuts, mayo) from New York's Waldorf Hotel in the 1890s. Swap the apples for blue cheese, and you get the modern version.

What we do know is that it became an American classic through steakhouses and sports bars — the places that embraced bold flavors. The original versions were simpler, but the American touch added creaminess and tang to make it a condiment that could stand up to strong greens and, later, Buffalo wings.

## Easy Variations

**Lighter Version:** Swap half the mayo for Greek yogurt. Still tangy, less heavy.

**Extra Tangy:** Add 1-2 extra teaspoons of vinegar. More bite.

**Bacon Upgrade:** Add 2 slices of cooked, crumbled bacon. Because obviously.
